L0 · Welcome notebook §0–1

What tight binding is, how the course runs, and the one Hamiltonian everything else is a special case of.

Key ideas

  • A crystal is 1023 atoms. Tight binding keeps one number per atom (an energy) and one number per bond (a hopping) — and still predicts metals, insulators, and topological matter.
  • Each lecture is one vertical stack of slides: a picture first, the physics next, the equations last. Notebook sections are given for every lecture.
  • The deck is a two-dimensional grid: → jumps between lectures, ↓ descends through one lecture from the simplest slide to the most mathematical.

Equations

  • H = Σi εi c†ici + Σ⟨ij⟩ ( tij c†icj + h.c. ) — real space
  • Hab(k) = ΣR e i k·(R + τb − τa) Hab(R) — Bloch Hamiltonian
  • H(k) un(k) = En(k) un(k) — eigenproblem

L1 · From atoms to bands notebook §1–2

A single orbital per atom, one hopping, and Bloch's theorem give the first band structure — and the first PythTB model.

Key ideas

  • The simplest crystal: a row of identical atoms, spacing a. Each has one orbital at energy ε0; an electron hops to the neighbour with amplitude t.
  • A PythTB model is a lattice (vectors + orbital positions) plus a table of onsite energies and hoppings. Nothing is solved until you ask.
  • Numerical eigenvalues along the 1D Brillouin zone lie exactly on the analytic cosine. Bandwidth 4|t|, minimum at k = 0.

Equations

  • |ψk⟩ = Σn e i k n a |n⟩ — Bloch state
  • E(k) = ε0 + 2t cos(ka) — dispersion
  • E ≈ ε0 + 2t + |t| a2 k2 ⇒ m* = ħ2 / (2|t|a2) — near the bottom

L2 · The SSH chain notebook §3, §6, §13

Two sites per cell teach dimerization, gap closing, end states and the winding number — the whole of topology in one dimension.

Key ideas

  • Polyacetylene-style: bonds alternate strong, weak, strong, weak. The unit cell holds two orbitals, at τ = 0 and τ = ½, joined by the intracell hop v; the intercell hop w joins cells.
  • PythTB lets a hopping be a parameter. The same model evaluated at w < v, w = v, w > v gives two gapped band structures and one metal in between.
  • The direct gap over the whole (k, w) plane vanishes only at w = v, k = π.
  • cut_piece makes a finite chain. For w > v two states sit in the middle of the gap, one per end, exponentially localized.
  • Fourier-transform the occupied Bloch band back to real space and you get a localized orbital — the Wannier function. Its centre is the position of the electron's charge.

Equations

  • H(k) = d(k)·σ, d(k) = ( v + w cos k, w sin k, 0 ) — Bloch Hamiltonian
  • E±(k) = ± |v + w eik| = ± √( v2 + w2 + 2vw cos k ) — bands
  • ν = (1/2π) ∮ dk ∂k arg( v + w eik ) ∈ {0, 1} — invariant

L3 · Graphene & flat bands notebook §4–7

Honeycomb Dirac cones, how a sublattice potential gaps them, exactly flat bands, ribbons, supercells and defects.

Key ideas

  • Graphene is a triangular lattice with a two-atom basis (A and B). Every A neighbours three B's. Its Brillouin zone is a hexagon whose corners K, K' will carry all the physics.
  • Left: graphene's bands touch at K and K' in linear crossings, coloured by sublattice weight. Right: boron nitride — same lattice, different atoms on A and B — opens a gap of 2Δ.
  • solve_mesh over a patch of the zone around K: the two bands form a double cone, isotropic to first order.
  • Three sites per square cell (one corner, two edges). One band has no dispersion at all — and a compact localized state explains why in one picture.
  • Corner-sharing triangles, three sites per cell. The spectrum has both a Dirac crossing at K and a flat band touching a dispersive one quadratically at Γ.
  • cut_piece along one direction makes a strip: dim_k = 1 inside dim_r = 2. Its band structure depends on the edge: the zigzag termination carries a flat edge band at E = 0.
  • make_supercell repeats the cell; the bands fold into a smaller zone (left). remove_orb deletes a site: a vacancy in graphene binds a zero-energy state on the other sublattice (right).

Equations

  • H(k) = ( Δ f(k) ; f*(k) −Δ ), f(k) = t Σj=13 e i k·δj — two-band model
  • E±(k) = ± √( Δ2 + |f(k)|2 ) — bands
  • f(K + q) ≈ ħvF (qx − i qy), ħvF = 3tacc/2 — near K

L4 · Spin, 3D, materials notebook §8, §11–12

Spinful models with Rashba and Zeeman terms, the simple cubic crystal and its density of states, and real silicon from Wannier90.

Key ideas

  • spinful=True doubles every orbital and lets hoppings be 2×2 matrices in spin space. A one-dimensional wire with Rashba spin–orbit coupling and a Zeeman field shows what that buys.
  • Colour the bands by ⟨σz⟩ (left) or plot the full texture ⟨σy⟩, ⟨σz⟩ along the band (right): the spin direction is fixed by the momentum.
  • One orbital, six equal nearest-neighbour hops. The band is a sum of three cosines, bandwidth 12|t|, checked analytically along a standard path.
  • Histogram 216 000 eigenvalues from a full 3D mesh: the density of states has kinks at E = ±2t where the band has saddle points.
  • Two interpenetrating fcc lattices, each atom tetrahedrally bonded to four neighbours. Tight-binding parameters for it can be computed, not guessed: Wannier90 turns a first-principles calculation into hoppings.
  • Every hopping matrix element versus distance (left): exponential decay. Keep the hoppings above a threshold and compare with the Wannier90 reference bands (right): the truncated model tracks the first-principles bands.

Equations

  • H(k) = −2t cos k + 2α sin k σy + B σz — Rashba–Zeeman wire
  • E±(k) = −2t cos k ± √( (2α sin k)2 + B2 ) — bands
  • g(E) = Σn ∫BZ d3k/(2π)3 δ( E − En(k) ) — density of states

L5 · Berry phase & pumping notebook §9–10

The Berry phase as the position of charge, polarization as a Wannier centre, and the Thouless pump as a quantized flow.

Key ideas

  • In an insulator the occupied band is a cloud of charge in each cell. Its centre — the Wannier centre — is the electric polarization. Move the atoms, and the centre moves by a definite amount.
  • Add a staggered onsite energy ±Δ to the SSH chain. In the plane of (dimerization, Δ) the gap closes only at the origin. A cycle that encircles it is a Thouless pump.
  • Track the Wannier centre as the pump parameter λ advances: it flows continuously across one full unit cell and returns to its starting value mod a. That integer is the pumped charge.
  • In a finite chain the pumped charge has to go somewhere: as λ advances, a bound state peels off the valence band at one end, crosses the gap and joins the conduction band — one state per cycle.

Equations

  • φ = i ∮ dk ⟨uk| ∂k uk⟩ = −Im ln Πj ⟨ukj| ukj+1⟩ — Berry phase of a band
  • P = e a φ / 2π (mod e a), x̄ = a φ / 2π — polarization
  • ΔQ = e ∮ dλ ∂λ(φ/2π) = e · C, C = (1/2π) ∫∫ dk dλ Ωkλ ∈ ℤ — Thouless pump

L6 · Chern insulators notebook §14

The Haldane model: Berry curvature, the Chern number, the phase diagram, chiral edge states and a real-space marker.

Key ideas

  • Graphene plus directed second-neighbour hoppings t2 eiφ, arrows clockwise around each hexagon. The net flux through the cell is zero — yet time reversal is broken.
  • The Berry curvature Ω(k) of the occupied band over the Brillouin zone. It is concentrated near K and K', where the gap is smallest — and it integrates to 2π times an integer.
  • Chern number over the (φ, M/t2) plane, computed point by point, against the analytic boundary M = ±3√3 t2 sin φ. Inside the lobes C = ±1; outside, C = 0.
  • Cut a ribbon in the C = 1 phase and colour the bands by edge weight: a single state crosses the gap on each edge, moving in opposite directions on opposite edges.
  • The Bianco–Resta marker evaluates C at each site of a finite flake from the projector onto occupied states and the position operators. In the bulk it reads the Chern number; at the edges it reverses sign so the total is zero.

Equations

  • Ω = ∂kxAy − ∂kyAx, A = i⟨u|∇ku⟩, C = (1/2π) ∫BZ Ω d2k ∈ ℤ — curvature and Chern number
  • C = (1/2π) Σplaquettes Im ln ( U12 U23 U34 U41 ), Uij = ⟨ui|uj⟩/|⟨ui|uj⟩| — lattice (Fukui–Hatsugai–Suzuki)
  • mK = M − 3√3 t2 sin φ, mK' = M + 3√3 t2 sin φ, C = ½[sgn(mK') − sgn(mK)] — Haldane masses

L7 · Z₂ & spin Hall notebook §15–16

Time reversal changes the rules: Kramers pairs, band inversion, the Z₂ invariant from Wannier flow, helical edges.

Key ideas

  • In a HgTe quantum well the ordering of the s-like and p-like bands at Γ flips with the well thickness. Colour the BHZ bands by orbital character: for M > 0 the characters are swapped near Γ — the inverted regime.
  • Hybrid Wannier centres x̄(ky) over half the zone, for Kane–Mele (left) and BHZ (right). In the topological phase the two centres switch partners: any horizontal line crosses them an odd number of times.
  • Ribbons in the topological phase carry, on each edge, two counter-propagating states with opposite spin (left, Kane–Mele). BHZ ribbons (right): edge states only in the inverted regime.

Equations

  • H = t Σ⟨ij⟩ c†icj + i λSO Σ⟨⟨ij⟩⟩ νij c†i sz cj + λv Σi ξi c†ici — Kane–Mele
  • Θ = i sy K, Θ2 = −1 ⇒ Kramers: En(k) = En'(−k), degenerate at TRIM — time reversal
  • ν = ( # crossings of x̄n(ky) with any line, ky ∈ [0, π] ) mod 2 = Πi=14 δi, δi = Πn occ ξ2n(Γi) (with inversion) — Z₂

L8 · Corners, Majoranas, Weyl notebook §17–20

Higher-order topology, superconductivity as extra orbitals, Weyl monopoles with Fermi arcs, and the axion angle.

Key ideas

  • The same machinery — a lattice model, its eigenvectors, a Berry-phase quantity — classifies far more than Chern and Z₂ insulators. This lecture is a tour; each stop is a full notebook section.
  • The Benalcazar–Bernevig–Hughes model has a gapped bulk, gapped edges, and four zero modes on the corners (left). The invariant lives one level down: the Wilson-loop eigenphases form Wannier bands, gapped and pinned at ±¼ by symmetry (right).
  • PythTB knows nothing about pairing. Double the orbitals — particles and holes — and write the Bogoliubov–de Gennes matrix as hoppings. Majorana end modes appear exactly for |μ| < 2t (left), one per end (right).
  • Two bands touch at isolated points in a 3D zone. Each node is a monopole of Berry curvature: the flux through any closed surface around it is ±2π. Nodes come in pairs of opposite charge.
  • Fix kz and you have a 2D insulator with a Chern number; C(kz) jumps by one at each node (left). Every slice with C = 1 has a chiral edge state — stacked along kz, they form a Fermi arc on the surface connecting the node projections (right).
  • The Fu–Kane–Mele model on the diamond lattice, driven through an adiabatic cycle by a staggered Zeeman field. The magnetoelectric angle θ winds by 2π over the cycle: a second Chern number equal to one.

Equations

  • H = ½ Σk Ψ†k HBdG(k) Ψk, E(k) = ± √( (2t cos k + μ)2 + 4Δ2 sin2 k ), topological for |μ| < 2t — Kitaev BdG
  • H(k0 + q) = χ ħ v q·σ, ∮S2 Ω·dS = 2π χ, χ = ±1 — Weyl node
  • θ = −(1/4π) ∫BZ d3k εabc Tr[ Aa∂bAc − (2i/3) AaAbAc ], Δθ over a cycle = 2π C2 — axion angle

L9 · Stretching PythTB notebook §21–24

Magnetic fields by Peierls phases, Anderson localization, a Penrose quasicrystal, and the O(N³) wall.

Key ideas

  • PythTB has no magnetic field. It does have complex hoppings. The Peierls substitution turns a uniform field into a phase on every bond — in Landau gauge, hops along x stay real and hops along y pick up e2πi φ x.
  • Every eigenvalue of the square lattice at every rational flux p/q with q ≤ 32, from set_hop and nothing else. Nested gaps at every scale; each gap carries a Chern number.
  • Random onsite energies on a 30×30 supercell. The inverse participation ratio of every eigenstate (left) separates extended from localized states; one band-centre state at weak versus strong disorder (right) shows what localization looks like.
  • De Bruijn's pentagrid generates a Penrose rhombus tiling; each vertex becomes a site, each rhombus edge a bond. Five-fold symmetry, no unit cell — a dim_k = 0 model with a few thousand orbitals.
  • The density of states is spiky, riddled with gaps, with a δ-peak at E = 0 that holds a finite fraction of all states (left). One such state (right) is strictly zero outside a few vertices: a confined state.
  • Wall time of a full dense diagonalization versus matrix size, log-log. The slope is 3: O(N3). PythTB has no sparse path — every solve_* call pays this.

Equations

  • tij → tij exp( i (e/ħ) ∫rirj A·dl ), Landau gauge A = (0, Bx, 0): ty(x) = t e 2πi φ x/a, φ = Ba2e/h — Peierls substitution
  • ψx+1 + ψx−1 + 2 cos( 2π φ x + ky ) ψx = (E/t) ψx, x = 1 … q — Harper equation (φ = p/q)
  • IPR(ψ) = Σi |ψi|4 / ( Σi |ψi|2 )2 ∼ N−1 (extended), ξ−d (localized) — inverse participation ratio

L10 · Limits & what next notebook §25–31

What PythTB cannot do (transport, sparse, continuum, symmetry rails, interactions), where it goes wrong silently, and the road onward.

Key ideas

  • Knowing a tool's edge is part of knowing the tool. Each of these is a notebook section with a worked demonstration of the limit — and a pointer to what to use instead.
  • A 'BdG Hamiltonian' whose hole block was deliberately corrupted. PythTB builds it, solves it, plots it — and the spectrum is no longer particle–hole symmetric. No error, no warning.
  • A self-consistent loop around PythTB: guess the local moments, build the spin-dependent onsite energies, solve, recompute the moments, repeat. The converged solution (left) shows opposite magnetization on the two zigzag edges, painted on the ribbon (right).
  • PythTB versus Kwant, honestly. Use both; know which one you are holding.

Equations

  • U ni↑ni↓ ≈ U( ⟨ni↓⟩ ni↑ + ⟨ni↑⟩ ni↓ − ⟨ni↑⟩⟨ni↓⟩ ), mi = ⟨ni↑⟩ − ⟨ni↓⟩ — Hubbard mean field
  • εiσ(n+1) = U ⟨ni,−σ⟩(n), ⟨niσ⟩ = ΣEα<EF |ψα(i,σ)|2, iterate until |m(n+1) − m(n)| < tol — self-consistency
  • G = (e2/h) Tr( t†t ) = (e2/h) Σn Tn, S = ( r t' ; t r' ) — Landauer–Büttiker (Kwant's problem)

Glossary

Tight-binding model
A Hamiltonian written in a basis of localized orbitals: onsite energies and hoppings, nothing else.
Bloch Hamiltonian H(k)
The finite matrix obtained by Fourier transforming the hoppings; its eigenvalues are the bands.
Brillouin zone
The unit cell of reciprocal space; k and k + G label the same state.
Reduced coordinates
Positions in units of the lattice vectors, k in units of the reciprocal vectors — PythTB's convention everywhere.
Atomic gauge
Bloch phases that include the orbital position τ; makes Berry phases equal to physical positions.
Chiral (sublattice) symmetry
An operator anticommuting with H; forces the spectrum symmetric about zero and protects SSH end modes.
Berry phase
The phase acquired by a band eigenvector transported around a closed loop in k; gauge invariant mod 2π.
Wannier centre
Berry phase divided by 2π, in units of the lattice constant: the position of the band's charge.
Berry curvature Ω
The local density of Berry phase in a 2D k-plane; its integral over the zone is 2π times the Chern number.
Chern number C
Integer invariant of a 2D gapped band; equals the Hall conductance in units of e²/h and the number of chiral edge modes.
Z₂ invariant ν
Parity-valued invariant of a time-reversal-symmetric 2D insulator; ν = 1 means one protected helical edge pair.
Kramers pair
Two degenerate states related by time reversal when Θ² = −1; cannot be split by any time-reversal-symmetric perturbation.
Wilson loop
The product of overlap matrices around a closed k-loop; its eigenphases are hybrid Wannier centres.
Bulk–boundary correspondence
A bulk invariant fixes the number of protected boundary states.
Bogoliubov–de Gennes (BdG)
The doubled particle–hole Hamiltonian that writes pairing as hopping; every eigenvalue appears with its negative.
Majorana mode
A zero-energy BdG state equal to its own particle–hole partner; two of them make one fermion.
Weyl node
A point band touching in 3D acting as a monopole of Berry curvature; nodes come in pairs of opposite chirality.
Axion angle θ
The 3D magnetoelectric Berry-phase angle; θ = π characterizes a strong topological insulator.
Peierls substitution
Multiplying each hopping by the phase e^{i(e/ħ)∫A·dl} to include a magnetic field.
Inverse participation ratio
Σ|ψ_i|⁴ for a normalized state; ~1/N for extended, ~1/ξ^d for localized states.
Supercell
A larger periodic cell built from copies of the primitive one; needed for defects, disorder and fields.
Density of states
The number of states per unit energy; its singularities (van Hove) mark saddle points of the bands.
